## 1. Is a feed measurement mismatch established, and what causes it?
Between production and the feed the model was fitted on: NO, under the documented fit construction. Rebuilding the fit-type index (Coinbase 1.0 / Kraken 0.8 last trade, 1 Hz, 30-s staleness) from the stored public trades of Nov 12–14 and applying the engine's own estimator to both, production's reading (row sigma ÷ 1.03) is 1.013 × the fit-type sigma at the median over 32,069 decision-seconds (p10 0.95, p90 1.07), 1.013–1.018 in the cells of the two live losses, +1 to +2 % per decision window, ±3 % day to day; with the 1.03 scale in force production reads about 4 % above the fit scale — slightly conservative. A reviewer reproduced the headline and tightened its band to about ±5 % in the loss regime.
Between the replay proxy and everything else: YES. The Binance single-venue last-trade series reads 1.25 × the fit-type index and 1.18 × the official BRTI at 1-s returns, decaying to 1.02 at 60 s: short-horizon microstructure noise (bid-ask bounce; 52 % of its seconds carry no trade) that the multi-venue indices and the live composite do not have. This is why the historical "0 in 7,281" cohort was stricter than production (rounds 5–6).
Why 1-s volatility differs between the composite and BRTI while 5-s agrees: the composite and the fit-type index incorporate a move over about two seconds (positive lag-1 autocorrelation +0.13 to +0.20; quote-based venues with 30-s staleness and liquidity weighting), BRTI less (+0.08; slightly negative in calm blocks); the pooled 1-s ratio is 0.95 and the per-window median 0.88 (calm windows 0.83, active 0.98); from 3 s on the two agree within 3 %, and the innovation of the 60-print settlement average agrees to 1.5 % (correlation 0.999). Missing prints (17 % of seconds, more on Nov 16) and the decision tick's phase (0.01–0.42 s into the second) do not change the ratios. The measurement appropriate for the settlement target is the model's own: the 1-s RMS of the composite as the engine computes it, because the calibration maps exactly that reading to real settlement outcomes; BRTI's 1-s RMS is not the quantity the tables were fitted to, and at the horizons that move the settlement average the two coincide.
Caveat that bounds the conclusion: the fit feed itself is gone (the raw 1 Hz store was lost with an earlier container; the construction is documented and reproducible; the fitting code never was in this container). A Coinbase-only fit feed would put production 12 % below the fit scale, a per-second VWAP feed 14 % above; nothing here distinguishes these from the documented construction, so the "no mismatch" finding is conditional on it.

## 2. Is a constant scale correction justified?
No. The measured ratio is 1.01 at the model's own estimator; its horizon and regime dependence (0.90 calm to 0.98 active at 1 s, 1.00–1.04 at 5–30 s) is not a constant, and no measurement supports a multiplier in either direction beyond the ±5 % band. A multiplier chosen for its effect on the known losses would be the tuning the owner ruled out. Section 27's LIVE_VOL_SCALE ≈ 1.17 hypothesis is refuted and withdrawn.
