## 2. What it indicates
(a) A DATA MISMATCH, established: volatility measured on a different feed; it removes one of the two live losses from the historical cohort and most of the rate gap. (b) A POSSIBLE MARKET CHANGE, supported but not established: inside the replay itself the loss rate of the same rule roughly doubled from September (all bins 0.086 % → 0.172 %, P = 0.025; 30–89 s 0.036 % → 0.167 %, P = 0.002; the comparable 90–120 s cohort 0.013 % → 0.139 %, P = 0.003), before the live period; the Nov 13–16 cluster continues that pattern rather than starting it. (c) A CONDITIONAL MODEL WEAKNESS only in the narrow sense that the display reads 0.2 % for a class whose comparable empirical loss frequency is 0.04 % (year) to 0.14 % (recent) historically and 0.48 % live — not an order of magnitude wrong, but with no margin to the break-even of a 99.8–99.9 ¢ purchase (0.18 % / 0.09 % at 100 contracts). (d) UNRESOLVED: whether the live engine's lower volatility reading is the better estimate of the settlement index's volatility (a composite like BRTI) or an under-reading, and whether the Nov 13–16 cluster is chance at the recent rate (P ≈ 0.12) or an excess (cushion-conditioned, post hoc, P ≈ 0.003–0.03). Two events cannot settle it.
