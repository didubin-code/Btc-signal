# Round 7, section 2 — The remaining settlement exposure, measured directly (2026-11-16)
All figures are reproducible from the scripts named; the files are under round7/exposure, round7/qtable and round7/eval.

## 2.1 The exposure at any second, in one number
With n official prints recorded (their sum enters the expected settlement exactly) and r = 60 − n still unknown, the settlement reverses against the leader only if the MEAN of the r unknown prints ends at least R = 60·|gap| / r below (UP) or above (DOWN) the current index; before the settlement minute (n = 0, h seconds to close) the mean of the 60 prints must move by R = |gap| against the leader over a horizon of h seconds. R is the "required adverse path"; it depends on the recorded prints exactly and on nothing else about the past. The engine already exposes it as `requiredMoveUsd` inside the minute (lockGeometry25). It contains no volatility estimate.

## 2.3 The same quantity at every second of three years, without windows (the exceedance table)
qtable/build_q_full.py: for each of the 92,016,000 seconds of 1-s BTC/USDC closes 2023-10-02 … 2026-09-01 and each horizon h = 1 … 120 s, the settlement-type mean over h (next h prints for h ≤ 60; the 60 prints ending at t + h for h > 60) minus the price at t. Q(R, h) = fraction of seconds with |move| ≥ R (two-sided; the adverse side is about half: signed tails are symmetric to within 10 %, qtable/q3_ztails.txt). Selected values: h = 120 s: R ≥ 15: 5.1 %, 20: 2.5 %, 30: 0.75 %, 45: 0.20 %, 60: 0.074 %, 68: 0.050 %, 100: 0.015 %; h = 90: 20: 1.3 %, 30: 0.35 %, 54: 0.05 %; h = 60: 20: 0.31 %, 35: 0.05 %; h = 30: 15: 0.27 %, 25: 0.05 %; h = 10: 10: 0.22 %, 16: 0.05 %. Year by year the 0.05 % threshold at h = 120 is 69/74/65/64 bps (2023/2024/2025/2026 Jan–Aug): the table is stable to ±8 %.

## 2.4 Dependence on time, cushion, recent movement, volatility and input uncertainty
* Time and cushion: above. Inside the minute the fall of risk with elapsed time is arithmetic (recorded prints cannot move) and the record confirms it; before the minute the fall of the engine's risk at a fixed cushion is the model's σ√h scaling and the history shows it is real but much flatter than Gaussian (2.5).
* Volatility: at a fixed cushion the all-rows loss rate at 90–120 s rises steeply with the engine's own regime bucket (FIT, cushion 15–20 bps: bucket 3: 0/662, 4: 1/1,073, 5: 7/1,155 = 0.61 %, 6: 34/975 = 3.5 %; exposure/bucket_crosscheck.py). Recent volatility is informative; the question is whether it is informative enough at the low end.
* Recent movement: the two live losses had a 20-s cushion change before entry of −1.0 and −8.1 bps against the 15 comparable winners' median +2.8 (p10 −3.9, p90 +11.8); BRTI age 126 and 336 ms vs winners' median 249; sigma change −0.06/−0.02 vs −0.02 (exposure/live_losses_vs_winners.txt). Nothing available at the entry second separates the two losses from the comparable winners beyond what two cases can show; round 4 reached the same conclusion on the replay losses (causal/matched.txt).
* Input uncertainty: production's recorded prints are official; the only input error is the composite index versus the next official prints (sub-bps at 1 s in the live rows, idx vs spot differing by 1–3 USD) and it enters the expected settlement scaled by r/60. The replay's error is the whole-minute basis (4–6 % of windows disagree at tiny margins). The live sensitivity test (eval/live_sensitivity.txt): making the index 3 bps less favourable removes 13 % of PR11-admitted row-seconds and 36 of 351 admitted windows; no admitted second had an official reference older than 2.5 s.

## 2.5 The hypothesis: low-risk readings driven by elapsed time and recent calm without accounting for jumps
How the engine reads risk (base_I2_pinradar_server.js, lockGeometry25 / m25PUp): path sigma = 1.03 × RMS of the 120-s and 900-s realised volatility of the 1 Hz index, in bps per √second; variance units of the 60-print mean from the exact discrete formula (m25VarUnits); the standardised residual is passed through a fitted power-law tail (settleTailSurvival, (1 + (z/0.804)²)^−1.268, two-sided: 0.94 % at z = 5) with a per-regime recalibration. It is therefore conditional on recent calm by construction, with a tail law fitted on the pooled basis windows.
Test 1 — volatility-normalised tails per engine bucket (qtable/q3_ztails.txt; z = |move| / (sigma_t × √V(h)) with each second's own engine-like sigma): at h = 120 the empirical P(z ≥ 5) is 0.57 % over all seconds (Gaussian 0.00006 %), 3.8 % in the calmest bucket 0, 1.3 % in bucket 1, 0.49 % in bucket 3, 0.33 % in bucket 4, 0.14 % in bucket 6. The pooled figure matches the engine's law (0.94 %, conservative); in the calmest buckets the real tail is 1.4–4× fatter than the law, in the active buckets thinner. Jumps do not shrink in proportion to recent calm: SUPPORTED, and it is strongest exactly where the engine's reading is lowest.
Test 2 — were the November readings wrong by the regime-conditional history? The two live losses were read at 0.188 % (cushion 14.9 bps, 91.8 s, bucket 3) and 0.199 % (18.3 bps, 101.6 s, bucket 4). The bucket-conditional exceedance (q3) gives, one-sided, about 0.12 % (bucket 3, R ≥ 15, h = 90) and 0.3–0.5 % (bucket 4, R ≥ 15–20, h = 105): the readings were of the right order by the regime history, and FIT's LOCKED record at those inputs was 0 of 1,267 windows. November's 2 of 30 at those inputs is an excess against both (P ≈ 0.001 against FIT; ≈ 0.01–0.05 against the bucket history) and ordinary only against the all-regime history (P ≈ 0.12 at 1.5–2 % one-sided).
Verdict: the hypothesis is supported as a mechanism (recent calm lowers the reading more than it lowers the jump risk, visibly in the calmest regimes), but it does not by itself explain November: the November reversals exceeded even the regime-conditional history. A policy that does not condition on recent calm (PR11) treats such entries as 1.5–2 % risks and refuses them; a policy that conditions on calm will refuse them only after a recalibration that no data in hand can validate against a cluster of two.

## 2.6 Losses versus comparable winners, using only entry-time information
Live (90–120 s, LOCKED ≤ 0.2 %): 151 entries, 2 losses; the losses' cells (cushion 10–20 bps, regime 3–4) hold 15 winners; the full table is in exposure/live_losses_vs_winners.txt. Replay (round 4, causal/matched.txt): 30 losses against 26,217 winners by standardised distance on (time left, cushion, sigma) with the regime bucket forced equal: no pre-entry feature separated them. The only quantity that separates the losses from the winners is the exposure R relative to the all-regime history: both live losses had R = 15–18 bps at h = 92–102 where R*(h) at 0.05 % is 55–60 bps; 94 % of the comparable winners also fall below R*(h) at their entry second, which is why PR11's protection costs most of the early entries (section 4).
